  rationale: The paper itself is not investable for a long-only Indian equity fund: wrong market, wrong asset classes, and it holds cash as a core mechanism. But the MECHANISM -- allocate dynamically across a small set of long-only sleeves using a trailing risk estimate, a hard risk cap and an l1 leash to a strategic mix -- is asset-agnostic. Our NIFTY500 factor sleeves are exactly such a set. This card tests the mechanism, not the paper.

  ASSUMPTIONS THE SOURCE PAPER MAKES THAT DO NOT HOLD HERE:
    - DIVERSIFICATION: the paper's lift depends on SPY/AGG/GLD being weakly or negatively correlated. Our six sleeves are single-country equity and correlate 0.74-0.94 pairwise. A risk-constrained optimiser over near-collinear assets has far less to work with; expect most of the paper's benefit NOT to transfer.
    - CASH: the paper de-risks into cash, and cash is where its drawdown control comes from. A long-only fully-invested equity mandate cannot do this. The mandate- compliant variant is therefore a materially different strategy.
    - RISK-FREE ASSET: no Indian cash-rate series is in the snapshot. Any cash-holding variant runs on a declared constant proxy and must be read under the rate sweep.
    - POINT-IN-TIME: NSE factor indices are backfilled to a 2005 base with methodology fixed much later. The sleeve definitions were chosen knowing this history.
    - RETURN BASIS: these are price-return indices. Dividends are missing from every series, so absolute returns understate and any cash-vs-equity comparison is biased against equity.
    - INVESTABILITY: an index is not a portfolio. Real sleeve replication carries tracking error, rebalance impact and sleeve-level turnover that the index does not charge.

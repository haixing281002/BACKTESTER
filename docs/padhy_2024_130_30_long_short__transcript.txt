=== PAGE 1 (p.210) ===
Analyzing the 130/30 Long-Short Strategy in Indian Stocks: A Comparative Study
Using Sensex 30 and Benchmark Returns. Prof. Amit Bathia, Mr. Charith Pechetti,
Ms. Ananya Gupta, Prof. Nandip Vaidya, Prof. Jagabandhu Padhy (corresponding
author) -- NMIMS Anil Surendra Modi School of Commerce, Mumbai. European
Economic Letters, ISSN 2323-5233, Vol 14, Issue 4 (2024).

Abstract: investigates the 130/30 long-short strategy in the Indian stock
market using a sample of 30 Sensex stocks, April 2019 to March 2024 (later
stated as September 2024 in Methodology), evaluated via Holding Period Return,
Sharpe Ratio and Information Ratio against Sensex benchmark returns. Finds the
strategy can outperform in certain conditions but is vulnerable in downturns;
rejects the null hypothesis.

Introduction: NSE third largest APAC exchange by market cap ($4648.41bn,
2024); Demat accounts 36m (Mar 2020) -> 160m (Jun 2024). 130/30 strategy per
Lo & Patel (2008): 130% long, 30% short, net 100% market exposure, allows
managers to express high-conviction long and short ideas simultaneously.

=== PAGE 2 (p.211) ===
Literature review citations: DiFonzo & Bordia (1998) on information ratio
>0.5 for long-short success; Qian & Hua (2005) on strategy risk from
variability in the information coefficient; Borchert & Zellmer-Bruhn (2010) on
volatility risk management; Hubner (2008) Generalized Treynor Ratio; Sharpe
(1994); Brush (1997) long-only vs long-short; Clarke et al. (2008) short
extensions; Tornberg (2009) 130/30 funds outperform long-only by 2-3%/yr;
Lo & Patel (2008): 130/30 generated 1.26% annualized return, info ratio 0.69;
Frino et al. (2011) momentum-based 130/30 in Australia; Briere & Szafarz
(2017) short-selling constraints and factor portfolios.

Research objectives: (1) analyse the 130/30 strategy on the 30 Sensex stocks
vs benchmark returns; (2) construct and critically evaluate the stock
selection criteria for the 130/30 strategy.

Null Hypothesis (H0): no significant difference between 130/30 strategy
returns and Sensex benchmark returns.

=== PAGE 3 (p.212) ===
Methodology: Data from Yahoo Finance, adjusted close price of the 30 Sensex
companies, April 1, 2019 to September 30, 2024. Risk-free government bond
price also collected. Benchmark = Sensex price over same period.

Data divided into 6-month periods -> 11 equal periods. Holding Period Return
(HPR) computed for all 30 stocks and the benchmark:
  HPR = (Price at end of 6 months - Price at start of 6 months) / Price at
  start of 6 months.

130/30 classification tools, each computed per 6-month period:
- Tracking Error = stdev(daily return of stock - daily return of benchmark
  index). Lower = more closely mimics benchmark.
- Information Ratio = Active Returns / Tracking Error, where Active Return =
  average return of portfolio - average return of benchmark. Higher = better
  risk-adjusted outperformance.
- Sharpe Ratio = (6-monthly return of portfolio - 6-monthly return of
  risk-free asset) / stdev(portfolio returns). Stdev computed in Excel.
- Treynor Ratio = (6-monthly return of portfolio - 6-monthly return of
  risk-free asset) / Beta of portfolio. Beta = covariance(benchmark, stock) /
  variance(market).

Classification criteria table (Figure 2):
  Tracking Error: Long = bottom 40% of stocks; Short = top 40% of stocks.
  Information Ratio: Long = top 40%; Short = bottom 40%.
  Sharpe Ratio: Long = top 40%; Short = bottom 40%.
  Treynor Ratio: Long = top 40%; Short = bottom 40%.
(The paper does not state how a stock scoring in the "long" cohort on some
metrics and "short" cohort on others is resolved -- i.e., no combination rule
across the four metrics is given beyond the table itself.)

Rationale given for 40%/40% split: avoids extreme-decile bias, captures
meaningful performance variation while keeping a sufficiently large,
diversified pool.

Stocks are classified in a 6-month window, then the SUBSEQUENT 6 months'
returns are used to evaluate performance against the benchmark (i.e., a
1-period-lagged formation-then-holding design).

=== PAGE 4 (p.213) ===
Data Analysis and Findings: Rs 1,00,000 notional invested in each strategy
and in the Sensex benchmark. Transaction cost: 26 bps, plus a short-selling
cost of 84 bps -- combined "1.1% of the net gain/loss value for the
classified stocks" applied to the 130/30 book. Only 26 bps transaction cost
applied to the Sensex benchmark leg (no shorting assumed there).

130/30 weighting: short book = -Rs 30,000 / (number of short-classified
stocks), equal-weighted (negative sign because shorting requires borrowing);
long book = Rs 1,00,000 / (number of long-classified stocks) -- note the text
says the long book is scaled by 1,00,000 but the worked long amount invested
in each period's table sums to Rs 1,30,000 (e.g. Figure 3: 5 long stocks x Rs
26,000 = Rs 130,000), i.e. the long-side notional is actually 130,000, not
100,000 as the prose states -- an internal inconsistency between the prose
weighting description and the worked Excel tables.

Year 2019-20: H1 (Apr-Sep 2019, formed on prior data unavailable / evaluated
Oct19-Mar20... paper's period labelling is formation-period = evaluation
period for the first row, see table): no stocks satisfied the short
criteria in the first half -- strategy "cannot be implemented" for that
window. Second half (formed Oct-Mar, evaluated Apr-Sep... per Figure 3's
literal rows: formation "2019-20 October-March", evaluation "2020-21
April-September"): 5 stocks long (RIL, TCS, Kotak Mahindra Bank, Asian
Paints, Tech Mahindra), 5 stocks short (ITC, Bajaj Finance, Axis Bank, Adani
Ports, IndusInd). Net gain/loss 130/30 = Rs 52,625.48 vs Sensex benchmark
profit Rs 34,590.57 -- 130/30 beats benchmark by ~Rs 18,035 (18.03 pp on
Rs 100,000 base).

=== PAGE 5 (p.214) ===
Year 2020-21 (Covid-19 period): formation Apr-Sep 2020, evaluated Oct20-Mar21:
Sensex profit Rs 27,867.72 (27.87%). 130/30: short book (ICICI Bank, NTPC,
Axis Bank) lost Rs 14,422; long book (RIL, HCL, Sun Pharma, M&M, Tata Motors)
gained ~Rs 48,698; net 130/30 gain after costs = Rs 33,898.96 (33.90%),
beating Sensex by ~Rs 6,031 (6.03 pp). Second half of 2020-21: no stocks
satisfied the short-classification criteria -> strategy not implementable
for that window.

Year 2021-22: first half -- no stocks satisfied the LONG criteria -> not
implementable. Second half (2nd Covid wave): Sensex loss Rs 5,240.80
(-5.25%). 130/30: short Asian Paints & IndusInd Bank, long Infosys & SBI;
net 130/30 loss Rs 16,474.76 (-16.47%), UNDERPERFORMING the benchmark by
~Rs 11,234 (-11.23 pp).

=== PAGE 6 (p.215) ===
Year 2022-23: both halves implementable. H1 (formed Apr-Sep22, evaluated
Oct22-Mar23): short Tata Motors, Power Grid, JSW Steel; long ICICI Bank, SBI,
ITC, Nestle. Net gain 130/30 = Rs 6,395.62 (6.40%) vs Sensex Rs 3,868.69
(3.87%) -- 130/30 beats by ~Rs 2,527 (2.53 pp). H2 (formed Oct22-Mar23,
evaluated Apr-Sep23): 130/30 gain Rs 14,304.57 (14.30%) vs Sensex Rs
11,343.08 (11.34%) -- beats by ~Rs 2,961 (2.96 pp).

=== PAGE 7 (p.216-217, text continues to Appendix) ===
Year 2023-24: H1 -- no stocks classified, strategy not implementable. H2
(formed Oct23-Mar24, evaluated Apr-Sep24): short Infosys, Bajaj Finance;
long RIL, Titan Company. Net 130/30 loss Rs 3,490.18 (-3.49%) vs Sensex gain
Rs 13,860.10 (13.86%) -- 130/30 UNDERPERFORMS by ~Rs 17,350 (-17.35 pp).

Figure 8 (Conclusion table), full year-by-year record (period, 130/30
return %, Sensex return %, difference pp):
  2019-20 Apr-Sep: no implementation
  2019-20 Oct-Mar: 52.63% vs 34.59% (+18.03 pp)
  2020-21 Apr-Sep: 33.90% vs 27.87% (+6.03 pp)
  2020-21 Oct-Mar: no implementation
  2021-22 Apr-Sep: no implementation
  2021-22 Oct-Mar: -16.47% vs -5.24% (-11.23 pp)
  2022-23 Apr-Sep: 6.40% vs 3.87% (+2.53 pp)
  2022-23 Oct-Mar: 14.30% vs 11.34% (+2.96 pp)
  2023-24 Apr-Sep: no implementation
  2023-24 Oct-Mar: -3.49% vs 13.86% (-17.35 pp)
(Of 10 half-year windows, only 6 had both a long and a short cohort and were
"implementable"; 4 had zero stocks in one leg and the paper reports the
strategy as simply not run for that window rather than substituting a
long-only or partial book.)

Conclusion: rejects H0 -- 130/30 returns are statistically distinct from
Sensex, but the direction is inconsistent (outperforms in some windows,
underperforms badly in others, notably the last reported window).

Limitations stated by the authors: (1) Covid-19 period volatility distorts
reliability; (2) the classification criteria FAILED TO IDENTIFY ANY STOCKS in
4 of 10 windows, i.e. the strategy could not always be run; (3) universe
restricted to Sensex 30 large-caps only, no mid/small-cap; (4) no
sector-relative benchmark check, only Sensex.

Future scope proposed by authors: extend to mid/small-cap; incorporate
behavioural-finance sentiment effects; use adaptive/ML-based stock-selection
criteria that update with regime changes.
